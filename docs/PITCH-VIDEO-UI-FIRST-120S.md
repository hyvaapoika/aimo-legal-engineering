## Objective and audience

Create a memorable two-minute Junction 2026 pitch for the Valio Aimo Trusted Trade Agents challenge. The film should feel useful, ambitious, warm and credible. First make the benefit clear to a chef, then reveal how the underlying gateway also serves external purchasing agents.

Core promise: **A kitchen expresses a need. Aimo helps turn it into a prepared purchase, with clear human control.**

The audience should remember four benefits:

1. Search in everyday language; products update while typing, without needing exact catalogue names.
2. Prepare repeat purchases from saved kitchen preferences instead of rebuilding each line.
3. Review an already-selected approved replacement when the preferred product is unavailable; retain exact product choices and editable quantities.
4. Use the same purchasing services through a familiar interface or an external agent, with defined permissions and human approval.

The product demonstration is the main evidence. Animation explains the opening problem and the background architecture. Do not show terminals, source code, developer tools or API payloads. Phone calls are discarded: no ringing, telephone scenes, spoken approval, call agents or phone-service branding. Live voice ordering is not a feature of this film.

## Art direction: playful editorial paper-cut animation

**Style sentence:** Playful editorial 2D motion design with textured paper-cut shapes, expressive chef characters, food-inspired colours, crisp typography, and satisfying object transitions. Charming and confident, with restrained humour. Move naturally between an illustrated kitchen and actual application footage.

### Look

- A miniature kitchen world assembled from layered cut-paper shapes. Use subtle grain and restrained paper shadows, not heavy distressed texture.
- Warm off-white background, deep Valio-inspired green, butter yellow and a few tomato-red/amber accents. Suggested palette: cream `#FAF7EF`, deep green `#284B3B`, butter `#F2D675`, tomato `#D9694A`, ink `#254047`. These are film art-direction choices, not an official brand specification.
- Simple, expressive chef character: rounded shapes, economical facial features, believable kitchen clothes, small eye/eyebrow and shoulder reactions. Humour comes from recognising the purchasing frustration.
- Ingredient characters may have personality through movement: a yoghurt carton slides away; butter and milk cards stay anchored; an approved alternative slots neatly into position.
- Use the supplied app logo/brand assets when available. Do not distort the logo, invent official endorsements or copy another studio's recognisable characters.
- Clean rounded sans-serif typography. Keep titles short and strong. Avoid filling scenes with explanatory paragraphs.

### Motion

- Use purposeful anticipation, quick movement and soft settling. Small amounts of squash/stretch and slight rotation give illustrated objects personality.
- Request notes scatter during the problem, then snap into organised cards when Aimo enters. A paper card can match-cut into a real UI card.
- Architecture is explained with a travelling request card, verified offer cards, a permission gate and a human approval stamp. The movement communicates the process.
- A warm approval-stamp “thunk”, a soft paper shuffle and a restrained confirmation chime can punctuate the story.
- During actual screen recordings, keep attention on the UI. Use a few deliberate crop/zoom moves and subtle cursor emphasis. Do not shake, warp, bounce or repaint the app. Never crop away qualifiers or obscure products, quantities and totals.
- Avoid robot chefs, glowing AI brains, flying source code, generic stock-business montages, distracting confetti and exaggerated savings counters.

## Screen-recording instructions for the person supplying footage

Record one continuous raw take through step 4, or supply four separate files named `00-search`, `01-request`, `02-products`, `03-basket`. Raw footage can be several minutes; only the final edit is two minutes. Record at normal speed with 3–5 second pauses on important states. Do not speak, add music or speed up the recording. Hide notifications. Use a landscape browser window, ideally 1920×1080 at 30 fps; keep text readable at normal browser zoom.

### Setup, before recording

- The app must be running locally. Open `http://localhost:3100/agent`, choose **Hotel breakfast** under **Kitchen**, then open **Preferences**.
- Review/save the starter preferences if needed: preferred yoghurt with an approved alternative, exact butter, exact milk. Close Preferences before filming. These are saved example preferences, not learned purchase history.
- For filming on 15 November 2026, use delivery **2026-11-16**, Monday. If recording later, choose a valid future date and update any corresponding spoken weekday in the edit.
- Rehearse once. Existing preferences, quantities, budgets and orders persist. Do not reset the database, loosen purchasing rules or invent an outcome for filming. If something fails, preserve the error and request help/replacement footage.

### Step 1 — plain-language discovery

Page: `http://localhost:3100/`

1. Start recording on the shop homepage. Click the main **Tell Aimo what you need** field.
2. Type **lactose-free milk, 1 litre** at normal speed. Do not press Enter.
3. Let the results update. Hold the query, relevant products, lactose-free filter, prices and availability readable in the frame.
4. Click the product name **Valio semi skimmed milk 1 l lactose free UHT**. Pause on its details. Click **Close panel**. Do not add it to the basket for this take.

Benefit: find products without knowing their exact supplier names; inspect price and stock in context. Keep search footage normal speed. Do not manufacture an instantaneous result. The browsing demonstration precedes the prepared-breakfast journey; it does not establish that a particular browsed item was ordered.

### Step 2 — prepare a familiar purchase

1. Click **Prepare my order** in the top navigation, or open `http://localhost:3100/agent`.
2. Select **Hotel breakfast**; leave Preferences closed after setup.
3. Enter: **Prepare our usual breakfast order for delivery 2026-11-16. Keep exact product choices and use only our approved alternatives.**
4. Pause for readability, click **Prepare basket** once, and record the actual processing through the result.

Benefit: reuse preferences instead of selecting each recurring product again. Processing waits may be shortened with an obvious cut or a brief “Preparation shortened” label, not a false speed claim.

### Step 3 — review the automatic alternative and edit a quantity

1. On the result, click **All ingredients** if rows are hidden.
2. Pause on **Breakfast yoghurt** and the approved-alternative note.
3. Open its product dropdown to show the preferred original marked **Unavailable**. Dismiss it without changing the approved selected alternative.
4. Show **Breakfast butter** and **Breakfast milk**, including their exact-choice labels.
5. Change milk **Packs** from **24** to **30**, then click outside the field. Wait for the quantity and total to update. If the saved starting count differs, increase it by one legal whole pack and capture the actual result instead.

There is no separate Swap button in this flow. The approved alternative is already selected. Record the suggestion and your ability to inspect/edit it.

Benefit: less replacement searching; exact choices preserved; the chef can correct the draft.

### Step 4 — familiar basket and submission

1. Click **Use this basket**.
2. Record **Your basket** on the normal shop, showing transferred products, revised quantity and total. Scroll slowly if necessary.
3. Click **Order** once. Capture the actual response.
4. Pause on **Sent for human review**, then stop the main recording.

Benefit: the prepared products become a purchasing proposal through familiar controls. Do not imply the held request has already become an approved order.

- No “revolutionary” or guaranteed-time-saving claim is needed. Let the interface and the shared-agent architecture demonstrate the ambition.
